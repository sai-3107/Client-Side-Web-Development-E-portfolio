Why early screening matters

Research centre

How the model was built, how well it performs, and where it should not be trusted. All figures come from the project notebook and the DiaBD CSV.

Data5,288 records, 14 features, binary target (6.5% diabetic)
CleaningGender encoded 0/1. Zeros in six physiological columns replaced by the column mean (one glucose value changed)
Split80/20 stratified, seed 42: 4,230 train, 1,058 test
ScalingStandardScaler fitted on training data only
ModelsLogistic regression, RFE (8 features), LassoCV, neural network (32-16-1)
Deployed14-feature logistic regression, running in your browser

Why logistic regression

All three models reach 93.5% to 93.7% accuracy, so the simplest one wins: its coefficients are readable, it runs without a server, and each prediction can be explained input by input.

Feature selection

RFE kept age, pulse rate, diastolic BP, glucose, weight, family diabetes, hypertension and family hypertension. LassoCV gave non-zero weight only to hypertension, glucose, weight, cardiovascular disease, diastolic BP and pulse rate.

Limitations

  • Trained on Bangladesh records and not validated on Indian patients. India’s diabetes prevalence (11.4%) is higher than this dataset’s 6.5%, so absolute probabilities may be understated for Indian users.
  • Only 342 diabetic cases, so subgroup estimates (stroke, heart disease) are very uncertain.
  • Family diabetes shows a negative association, which is probably a data collection artefact and not biology.
  • Associations are not causes. The model predicts who looks like past diabetic patients.
  • Modest discrimination (AUC 0.83). It will miss some cases and flag some healthy people.

Ethics and privacy

  • Estimates run in your browser. Nothing is sent to a server.
  • Saved reports live in this browser’s local storage and can be cleared at any time.
  • The tool supports a conversation with a doctor. It does not diagnose or advise treatment.
  • Risk bands used in this project: low under 35%, moderate 35% to under 70%, and high 70% or more.

Frequently asked questions

Short answers to what people ask first.

Is this a diagnosis?

No. It estimates how closely your numbers resemble people with diabetes in the dataset. Only a blood test ordered by a clinician can diagnose diabetes.

What glucose value should I enter?

Use your most recent blood glucose reading. Indian labs usually report mg/dL, which is the default here. The model works in mmol/L internally, and you can switch units at any time.

My result is low but I feel unwell. What now?

See a doctor. The model misses a large share of cases, and symptoms matter more than any estimate.

Was this trained on Indian data?

No. It was trained on the DiaBD dataset from Bangladesh. South Asian populations share many risk patterns, but the model has not been tested on Indian patients. Use it as a prompt to get tested, not as a verdict.

Where does my data go?

Nowhere. The calculation runs on your device, and saved reports are stored only in your browser.

Why can my result differ from another tool?

Tools are trained on different populations. This one learned from Bangladeshi patient records, so it may not transfer to other groups.
